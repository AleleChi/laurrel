## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Objectives

Following the rejection of the generic Cleaneva/Whitesill redesign, Laurrel Services was restored to its approved two-column Solshine layout. However, a critical review of the baseline copy identified significant issues:
1. **Unverified Claims & Fabrications:** Claims regarding non-toxic proprietary formulations, 24/7 dedicated dispatch, enterprise warehouse footprints, and unverified guarantees violated project rules.
2. **Abstract B2B Jargon:** Language such as "Single-point accountability", "Transparent scoping", and "SLA-backed performance" alienated residential homeowners, renters, and property managers.
3. **Repetition Across Services:** Cleaning, moving, and storage descriptions frequently recycled the same generic phrases ("high quality", "care and attention") without detailing actual operational scopes.
4. **Lack of Local Relevance:** The copy lacked grounding in Ottawa realities—such as synchronized 1st-of-the-month lease turnovers, bilingual condominium moving rules, Winter snow conditions, and lease gap holding periods.

The rewritten copy adopts a **direct, calm, practical Canadian voice**. It clearly articulates what Laurrel does, how the three services work independently or in combination, and how clients can plan their services accurately.

## 3. Style Guide & Voice Rules for Future Content

1. **Be Specific, Not Grandiose:** Write "We bring furniture blankets and floor runners", not "We utilize cutting-edge protective apparatus".
2. **Respect the Ottawa Context:** Recognize that many Ottawa clients are renters moving on the 1st or 15th, federal public servants, families moving between suburbs (Kanata, Orleans, Barrhaven), or homeowners renovating older properties in the Glebe or Westboro.
3. **No Unverified Accreditations:** Never claim BBB ratings, WSIB registrations, eco-certifications, or warehouse facility square footage without verified documentation from the business owner.
4. **Active, Clear Verbs:** Use "book", "move", "clean", "store", "protect", "confirm" rather than passive corporate constructions.
